# Findings: ideas i002-i009 (2026-10-02 / 03)

Two pipeline runs, eight ideas, none passed feasibility, so nothing reached validation, the
holdout (data from 2025-05-01 on is still unseen) or incubation. Everything below uses
development data only (before 2025-05-01). Each idea's cards, code, results and reports are in
`research/ideas/` and `reports/research/`; the extra analyses quoted here are reproducible with
the scripts in `analysis/` (run from the repo root, e.g.
`.venv/Scripts/python.exe research/findings/analysis/run_1h_claims.py`).

Strategy settings tried (the deflated-Sharpe trial count): 221 before these runs, 276 after the
4h/1d run, **339** after the 1h run.

## Run 1 (4h / 1d): i002-i004

**i003 is the best candidate so far, and it is largely long crypto.** Compared with holding the
same coin at the same risk (always long, same 25% volatility target), median across the ten
coins (`analysis/i003_vs_hold.py`, `analysis/i003_vs_voltarget_hold.py`):

- Sharpe 0.95 against 0.77; better on 6 of 10 coins.
- Kept 63% of the up months but only 45% of the down months: the genuine trend-following part.
- Return after removing market exposure about 9% a year, statistically clear only on BTC and SOL;
  about zero on BNB, XRP and LINK, negative on LTC.
- It loses in falling years (2022: -15% against -26% for same-risk holding) and makes less than
  same-risk holding in the bull years 2023 and 2024. Its timing value is mostly losing less in
  crashes. The development data has four rising years and one real falling year, which flatters
  any long-leaning rule.

**No simple fix for i003's short side** (`analysis/i003_short_patterns.py`, i003 v1's own 759
short trades): shorting only in downtrends still lost (coin below its 200-day average: 408
trades, -0.4% a trade; coin and BTC both below: 258 trades, -0.4%). The losses are whipsaws:
shorts that lasted over 30 days made +6.1% a trade (103 trades), the other 656 lost -1.3% a trade,
and nothing visible at entry separates them. The only positive slice (7-day funding in its top
third, +0.1% a trade) was one of about a dozen looked at.

**i004 (shorting crowded funding) failed every check.** After crowding starts, prices kept
rising: funding received (+0.34% a trade) covered a quarter of the price loss (-1.45% a trade),
and shorting at crowding onsets was worse than shorting at random times.

## Run 2 (1h): i005-i009

Core claims at each card's pre-registered settings (`analysis/run_1h_claims.py`). "Gross" is the
price move only; costs are 0.08% a side (0.16% a round trip) plus funding. The 95% ranges come
from resampling whole days, because the ten coins move together.

| Idea | Core claim | Gross per trade (95% range) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| i005 | After a high-funding settlement the price rebounds for 1-2 hours | +0.22% [+0.11, +0.33], positive on 10/10 coins | Real, but net +0.01% [-0.10, +0.12] |
| i006 | Breakout direction after a squeeze predicts which side of the bracket is hit | target hit 34.5% [32.5, 36.4] vs 33.3% by chance | Not supported |
| i007 | A break beyond open +- k x range continues | +0.12% [-0.00, +0.25]; 2022-25 only +0.01% [-0.13, +0.13] | There in 2019-21, gone since |
| i008 | A big 08:00-10:00 New York move continues through the session | -0.13% [-0.35, +0.11]; random timing beat it 97% of the time | Not supported |
| i009 | Big weekend moves reverse on Monday | -0.13% [-0.97, +0.69] | Not supported; market-wide weekend moves tended to continue |

Break-even cost per side (gross / 2): i005 about 0.11% with correctly booked funding, i006
0.07%, i007 0.06%, against 0.08% charged.

## Lessons

1. **On 1h, costs decide.** The real effects found were 0.10-0.25% a trade against a 0.16%
   round trip. An hourly strategy needs a much bigger edge per trade or cheaper execution
   (Binance's base-tier maker fee is 0.02% against 0.05% taker, but limit orders do not always
   fill and the fills that happen are the worse ones, so a maker-fill backtest flatters itself).
2. **The edges were mostly 2020-21.** i006 gross 0.38% a trade in 2021, about 0% in 2023-25;
   i007 0.30-0.35% in 2020-21, about 0% from 2022; most i005 signals fall in 2020-21.
3. **Coin selection is the biggest overfitting trap.** SOL was the best coin for i003, i006 and
   i008. i006 on SOL alone was profitable in all 27 grid settings and in both halves of the data
   (`analysis/i006_sol.py`), yet its deflated Sharpe falls from 0.95 (as if SOL had been chosen in
   advance) to 0.07 once the pick of the best of ten coins is counted (339 trials;
   `analysis/i006_sol_deflated.py`). The coin list itself is chosen with hindsight: SOL is in it
   because it became huge.
4. **Beating the monkeys is not enough.** i005-i007 beat almost all random-timing monkeys and
   still lost money, because the monkeys pay the same costs. A same-risk buy-and-hold benchmark
   check is still to be built.
5. **Post-hoc leads, not evidence**: Monday longs (i008), market-wide weekend moves continuing
   (i009), SOL's breakouts (i006). Each would need a new pre-registered card and data it has not
   seen (the holdout, or the 38 USDC coins).

## Method changes made along the way

- Entry test scoring is set by `feasibility.entry_scoring` in `criteria.yaml` (32d53ae); the
  user chose Davey's fixed-size scoring (a6552cb). The fixed-size count no longer stops after a
  trade that would wipe out a 1x account (6bddc94). i002 and i003 were judged under the old
  compounded scoring; i004-i009 under fixed.
- The stake never exceeds the size with the highest median Monte Carlo return (6bddc94).
- `criteria.yaml` (c670247, at the user's request): 1h allowed; ruin = losing 70% (was 50%);
  median drawdown limit 55% (was 40%); stake multipliers up to 3x. Claude may edit the file only
  with the user's approval (an `ask` permission rule); pipeline stages never edit it.
- 1h support for the NautilusTrader node and parity check (6bddc94).
- Found, not yet fixed on main: about 43% of funding settlements are booked one bar late, because
  Binance stamps them a few milliseconds after the hour. Not lookahead; about 0.04% a trade on
  i005, no verdict changes. Being fixed on the `fix/funding-alignment` branch.

## Suggested next steps

- Back to 4h/1d, where costs matter much less: a Carver-style blend of i003-style long-only trend
  with an uncorrelated rule, judged against the same-risk buy-and-hold benchmark.
- Only if pursuing 1h: i005 is the one effect with statistical support; it needs a realistic
  limit-order fill model before it can be judged.
